{lead}The progress file is how the person stays aligned with you while you work: {path}

Add a note only if something happened that they would want to know: a piece is finished and can be looked at, you took a decision or made an assumption on their behalf, your understanding of what they want changed, or something is blocked or waits for them. If none of that happened since your last note, write nothing and carry on.

When you write, add a section at the end of that file with your edit tool, under a heading with the time and a title in plain words. Cover what applies:
- What changed for them: what they can now see or do, and where. Not which files you touched.
- What you decided and why, and what you assumed without asking them.
- How you understood what they asked, where that was open to more than one reading.
- What is running or half done, and what comes next.
- What you need from them: a decision, a look, an answer. Say so plainly when it is nothing.

Write it the way you write to them in the conversation: in the language they write to you in, in plain complete sentences. The reader did not watch you work: no task numbers, and no internal name or abbreviation without saying what it is. As long as the matter needs and no longer.
